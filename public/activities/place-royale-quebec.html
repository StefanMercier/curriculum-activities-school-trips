Presentation Overview

Sponsored by Global Educational Tours, this interactive presentation explores the birthplace of French America through student role-play at historic Place Royale in Quebec City.

Student Roles

1. Samuel de Champlain

Introduction (Hook):

"The Father of New France. I founded Quebec City in 1608 and established the first permanent French settlement in North America at Place Royale."

Content:
  • 1608 founding of Quebec
  • Strategic location at river's narrowing
  • Trading post establishment
  • Relations with indigenous peoples
  • Vision for New France colony
Conclusion:

"Place Royale became the heart of French commerce in America. From this small trading post grew an empire that stretched across the continent."

2. French Merchant

Introduction (Hook):

"A fur trader who built fortune and fame in New France. Place Royale was our marketplace, where European goods met North American furs."

Content:
  • Fur trade economics
  • Ships from France bringing supplies
  • Winter survival challenges
  • Relationships with coureurs de bois
  • Place Royale as commercial center
Conclusion:

"Without Place Royale's commerce, New France could not survive. This square was the lifeline between Europe and the New World."

3. Indigenous Guide

Introduction (Hook):

"Long before Champlain arrived, this was Stadacona, our seasonal fishing place. We taught the French how to survive in our homeland."

Content:
  • Pre-contact use of the site
  • Trade partnerships with French
  • Cultural exchange and adaptation
  • Knowledge of land and seasons
  • Changing relationships over time
Conclusion:

"Place Royale tells both French and Indigenous stories. Our peoples shaped this place together, though not always as equals."

4. Modern Historian

Introduction (Hook):

"An archaeologist uncovering Place Royale's layers of history. Each artifact tells us how people lived, worked, and survived here for centuries."

Content:
  • Archaeological discoveries
  • Reconstruction of historic buildings
  • UNESCO World Heritage designation
  • Preservation of French colonial architecture
  • Tourism and historical interpretation
Conclusion:

"Place Royale is North America's oldest commercial district. Through careful preservation, we keep the birthplace of French America alive for future generations."

Printable Student Scripts

"Here, at Place Royale, began the great adventure of French America, where two worlds met and a new society was born."

Samuel de Champlain

"I chose this spot where the great river narrows - 'kebec' in the local tongue. From here, we could control all river traffic and establish a permanent foothold for France in the New World."

French Merchant

"Every spring, ships from France arrived at Place Royale with iron tools, cloth, and wine. We loaded them with beaver pelts worth fortunes in European markets. This square was our gateway to prosperity."

Indigenous Guide

"Our ancestors fished here for countless generations. When the French came, we shared our knowledge of the land and seasons. Place Royale became a meeting ground of two worlds."

Modern Historian

"Beneath these cobblestones lie 400 years of history. Through archaeology and careful restoration, we've preserved the birthplace of French civilization in North America for all to experience."

Interactive Elements

Audience Questions

The St. Lawrence River narrows here ('kebec' means 'where the river narrows'), making it easy to control river traffic and trade.

It was New France's main commercial center - the marketplace where European goods were traded for North American furs and resources.

Archaeological excavations, historical documents, and preserved colonial architecture help us understand how people lived and worked here.

Educational Objectives

  • Understand the founding of New France and Quebec City
  • Explore the importance of the fur trade in colonial development
  • Recognize Indigenous contributions to early colonial survival
  • Analyze how historical sites preserve and interpret the past
