Student Roles

1. Washington Historian

Introduction (Hook):

"On April 30, 1789, George Washington stood on the balcony of Federal Hall in New York City to take the first presidential oath of office, establishing traditions that would shape the American presidency for centuries to come."

Content:
  • Washington's leadership during the Revolutionary War
  • His presidency from 1789 to 1797
  • The establishment of presidential traditions and protocols
  • Washington's decision to step down after two terms
  • His legacy as "Father of His Country"
  • Early efforts to memorialize Washington after his death
Conclusion:

"Washington's leadership and character established the foundation for the American presidency and made him the natural choice for the nation's first memorial to a citizen."

2. Symbolism Specialist

Introduction (Hook):

"The Washington Monument takes the form of an Egyptian obelisk, but what does this ancient symbol represent and why was it chosen to honor America's first president?"

Content:
  • Obelisks as ancient Egyptian symbols representing rays of sunlight
  • How obelisks honored pharaohs as divine rulers
  • Why this symbol was appropriate for Washington despite his rejection of monarchy
  • Alternative designs considered (equestrian statues, Greek temples)
  • Robert Mills' winning design
  • The Bunker Hill Monument as precedent
"The Washington Monument stands as a pillar of light, honoring the enlightenment Washington brought to the nation."
Conclusion:

"The obelisk form represents a ray of sunlight shining upon our most important historical figure, symbolizing the enlightenment and guidance Washington provided to the young nation."

3. Construction Historian

Introduction (Hook):

"If you look closely at the Washington Monument, you can see a visible change in the marble about one-third of the way up—a permanent reminder of the monument's troubled construction history."

Content:
  • Construction beginning in 1848 under architect Robert Mills
  • Funding challenges that halted construction in 1854
  • The 25-year construction hiatus during the Civil War era
  • Resumption of construction in 1879 with marble from a different quarry
  • The color variation resulting from different stone sources
  • Completion and dedication in 1884
"The two-toned monument tells a story of national division and eventual reunification, mirroring America's own history."
Conclusion:

"During our country's centennial anniversary, renewed public interest enabled construction to begin again, resulting in the iconic monument we see today as a fitting tribute to Washington."

4. Engineering Expert

Introduction (Hook):

"When completed in 1884, the Washington Monument was the tallest structure in the world—an engineering marvel that still holds records today."

Content:
Engineering Marvels:

• Tallest stone masonry structure in the world (555 feet, 5 ⅛ inches)

• Weighs approximately 90,854 tons

• Contains 898 steps in the interior stairwell

• Aluminum apex was the largest single piece of aluminum cast at the time

• The obelisk rests on an artificially constructed knoll for stability

  • Original location vs. final placement due to soil instability
  • The Jefferson Pier marking the originally intended location
  • The aluminum apex's function in lightning protection
  • 2011 earthquake damage and subsequent renovations
  • Current elevator and security improvements
Conclusion:

"The Washington Monument represents not only a tribute to our first president but also a remarkable feat of 19th-century engineering that continues to awe visitors more than a century after its completion."

Printable Student Scripts

Washington Historian

"George Washington served as the first U.S. President from 1789 to 1797 after leading the Continental Army to victory in the Revolutionary War. His leadership established crucial precedents for the presidency, including the two-term limit. Known as the 'Father of His Country,' Washington's legacy made him the natural choice for the nation's first major memorial to a citizen, with planning beginning shortly after his death in 1799."

Symbolism Specialist

"The Washington Monument takes the form of an Egyptian obelisk, which ancient civilizations used to represent rays of sunlight and to honor their pharaohs. Though Washington rejected monarchy, the obelisk symbolically represents the light of leadership he provided to the new nation. Architect Robert Mills' design was selected from numerous proposals, including equestrian statues and Greek temples, with the obelisk form seen as most appropriate for representing Washington's enlightened leadership."

Construction Historian

"Construction on the Washington Monument began in 1848 but halted in 1854 when funding ran out. The Civil War further delayed progress for 25 years. When construction resumed in 1879, marble came from a different quarry, creating the visible color change we see about one-third of the way up. Finally completed in 1884, the two-toned monument tells a story of national division and eventual reunification, mirroring America's own history during this period."

Engineering Expert

"The Washington Monument was the world's tallest structure when completed in 1884, and remains the tallest stone masonry structure today at 555 feet. Weighing 90,854 tons, it contains 898 steps and features an aluminum apex that was the largest single cast piece of aluminum at the time. The monument rests on an artificial knoll for stability, slightly offset from its originally planned location due to soil conditions. Despite damage from a 2011 earthquake, it continues to undergo renovations to preserve this engineering marvel for future generations."

Interactive Elements

Audience Questions

The color change results from a 25-year construction hiatus. When work resumed in 1879, marble came from a different quarry than the original 1848 stone, creating the visible variation about one-third of the way up the monument.

The Washington Monument is the tallest stone masonry structure in the world at 555 feet, 5 ⅛ inches. It was also the world's tallest structure overall when completed in 1884, before being surpassed by the Eiffel Tower in 1889.

Obelisks symbolized rays of sunlight in ancient Egypt, representing enlightenment and divine guidance. Architects felt this appropriately symbolized the leadership and vision Washington provided to the new American nation, despite his rejection of monarchy.

Activity: Monument Design Challenge

Have students work in small groups to:

  1. Research different monument styles from various cultures
  2. Brainstorm what symbol would best represent a modern leader
  3. Design their own monument to a historical figure of their choice
  4. Present their design to the group with explanation of its symbolism
  5. Discuss how monuments shape our understanding of history
Fascinating Washington Monument Facts

The monument's foundation is 37 feet deep
The walls are 15 feet thick at the base
The elevator ride to the top takes approximately 70 seconds
The original cost was $1,187,710
