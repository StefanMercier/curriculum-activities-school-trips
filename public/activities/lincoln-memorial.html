Presentation Overview

Sponsored by Global Educational Tours, this interactive presentation brings Lincoln's legacy to life through student role-play at America's most iconic memorial.

Student Roles

1. Abraham Lincoln

Introduction (Hook):

"The rail-splitter who saved the Union. I rose from a one-room cabin to the presidency, holding our nation together through its darkest hour."

Content:
  • Childhood in Kentucky log cabin
  • Self-taught lawyer in Illinois
  • 1860 election on anti-slavery platform
  • Civil War leadership decisions
  • Emancipation Proclamation
Conclusion:

"With malice toward none, with charity for all, I sought to bind up the nation's wounds - until that fateful night at Ford's Theatre."

2. John Wilkes Booth

Introduction (Hook):

"A famous actor whose hatred of Lincoln led to the most shocking crime in American history. My single act changed the course of Reconstruction."

Content:
  • Maryland theater family background
  • Confederate sympathizer during Civil War
  • Plot to kidnap, then kill Lincoln
  • April 14, 1865 - Ford's Theatre
  • 12-day manhunt and death
Conclusion:

"'Sic semper tyrannis' - thus always to tyrants. My deed intended to save the South but doomed it to harsher Reconstruction."

3. Memorial Designer

Introduction (Hook):

"Daniel Chester French and I created this temple to democracy, where Lincoln sits eternally watching over the nation he preserved."

Content:
  • 1922 dedication ceremony
  • 36 columns for states in Union
  • 19-foot seated Lincoln statue
  • Gettysburg Address and Second Inaugural carved in stone
  • Alignment with Washington Monument
Conclusion:

"Every detail honors Lincoln's legacy - from the marble to the murals, this memorial calls Americans to their highest ideals."

4. Civil Rights Witness

Introduction (Hook):

"This memorial became the stage for America's ongoing struggle for equality, from Marian Anderson in 1939 to Dr. King in 1963."

Content:
  • Marian Anderson's Easter concert, 1939
  • March on Washington, 1963
  • Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream"
  • Lincoln's unfinished work on equality
  • Modern gatherings for justice
Conclusion:

"Lincoln freed the slaves, but the memorial reminds us that the work of freedom is never finished. Each generation must claim it anew."

Printable Student Scripts

"In this temple, as in the hearts of the people for whom he saved the Union, the memory of Abraham Lincoln is enshrined forever."

Abraham Lincoln

"Born in poverty, I educated myself by firelight. As president, I faced an impossible choice: preserve the Union or end slavery. The Emancipation Proclamation did both, though it cost 620,000 lives. I died believing we could heal as one nation."

John Wilkes Booth

"Fame brought me close to power, hatred brought me to murder. I thought killing Lincoln would restore the old South, but my bullet only ensured harsher Reconstruction. One mad moment destroyed two lives and wounded a nation."

Memorial Designer

"We built this temple on the National Mall's axis, where Lincoln's gaze meets Washington's obelisk. The 36 Doric columns represent states in 1865. Inside, Lincoln sits in contemplation, hands resting - not in triumph, but in sorrowful wisdom."

Civil Rights Witness

"When Marian Anderson was banned from Constitution Hall, she sang here instead to 75,000 people. Twenty-four years later, Dr. King stood on these same steps, invoking Lincoln's promise that 'all men are created equal.' The memorial echoes with freedom songs."

Interactive Elements

Audience Questions

Daniel Chester French designed Lincoln to show the weight of leadership during civil war - contemplative and sorrowful, not triumphant.

The Gettysburg Address on the south wall and Lincoln's Second Inaugural Address on the north wall - his greatest speeches about unity and healing.

The Greek temple design honors Lincoln as a martyr for democracy, making the memorial a sacred space for American ideals.

Educational Objectives

  • Understand Lincoln's role in preserving the Union
  • Analyze the causes and consequences of assassination
  • Explore how memorials shape historical memory
  • Connect Lincoln's legacy to ongoing civil rights struggles
